# Could this be an HMO? HMO vs 2-bed house

See `layouts-2bed-vs-hmo.svg` / `.png` for notional layouts (concept only, not fitted to the site).

## Short answer
An HMO could be proposed, but I would not expect it to help. Everything the officer is worried about
(light to No.1, building line, slope, parking) gets harder when more unrelated adults live in the same
footprint, and the Council already says it is "yet to be convinced". It is also a different application
to describe honestly: it must say what use is intended.

## Facts to check (not verified; confirm with Manchester City Council)
- A small HMO (3–6 unrelated people sharing) is use class C4; more than six is sui generis. A new-build
  house is C3 unless it is applied for as an HMO. Changing C3 to C4 is normally permitted development, **unless
  an Article 4 direction removes it**. I do not know whether M9 8JT is covered. Ask the Council.
- Do not build as a house with a plan to convert quietly: declare the intended use.
- Licensing: an HMO for five or more people from two or more households needs a mandatory licence. The Council
  may have additional licensing for smaller HMOs. Check.
- Standards: the national minimum licensing bedroom sizes are 6.51 m² for one adult and 10.22 m² for two.
  The Council has its own amenity standards for kitchens, bathrooms and shared rooms (not checked).
- Parking, bins, noise and cycle storage are scrutinised more for an HMO.

## Comparison
| | 2-bed house (2b4p) | HMO (4 people) |
|---|---|---|
| Use class | C3 | C4 (declare it) |
| Floor area in the notional layouts | about 80 m² (NDSS minimum 79 m²) | about 80 m² |
| Bedrooms | 2 doubles, each at least 11.5 m² | 4 single, about 7.4–11.5 m² (meets 6.51 m² licensing minimum; most are below 7.5 m²) |
| Impact on No.1 | same footprint and height | same footprint; more people means more use of windows, noise, bins |
| Parking and bins | one space and normal bins | officer and highways likely to want more |
| Outdoor space | small yard or none (your wish) | same, but more occupiers |
| Planning risk | high (slope, building line, daylight) | higher (same plus Article 4, amenity, licensing) |
| Income | one letting or a sale | rooms let separately, usually more rent |
| Recommendation | **lead scheme** | only if the Council confirms no Article 4 and accepts it in principle |

## What to ask the officer
1. Is the site within an Article 4 direction for HMOs?
2. Would the Council consider a small HMO here at all, or only a family house?
3. What parking and amenity standards would apply?
