## 1. Does it fit the size? (space standard)
- 4b5p two-storey needs at least 97 m² GIA. The notional plan (`layouts-4bed.svg/.png`) gives about 98 m².
- It keeps the same depth (5.5 m internal) and widens the 2-bed house from 7.3 m to 8.9 m internal (about 8.0 to 9.5 m

- The ground-floor bedroom (single) is deliberate: it lets four bedrooms fit in a house this shallow.

## 2. Does it pass the daylight test? (BRE 209, 25° test, thresholds only)
The 25° test: obstruction angle = atan((height − window centre) ÷ distance). It passes at 25° or below, so the minimum
clear distance for a window directly opposite the new wall is (height − window centre height) ÷ 0.466.

Assumed heights (not surveyed): eaves 5.0 m, ridge 7.2 m above the new house's ground; kitchen window centre 1.7 m,
bedroom window centre 3.95 m above No.1's ground. If the plot's ground is higher or lower than No.1's, add or subtract the
difference.

| No.1 window | Distance needed to eaves | Distance needed to ridge |
|---|---|---|
| Kitchen (ground floor) | about 7.1 m | about 11.8 m |
| Bedroom (first floor, only window) | about 2.3 m | about 7.0 m |

What this means:
- The kitchen window is the critical one if the house stands in front of it. A 4-bed house that close fails unless it is
  more than about 7 m away or sits to the side so it does not block the view.
- The bedroom window clears the eaves at a modest distance, but the roof slope and ridge need about 7 m.
- If the house sits beside, not in front of, the gable (south-west of it), the 25° test is replaced by the Vertical Sky
  Component test. I can't run that without the window and house positions.
- The kitchen has other light (living room and back door), which helps the room as a whole, but BRE still tests each
  window. The bedroom has no other window, so it matters more.

## 3. What would make the 4-bed more likely to pass
- Keep two storeys with a low eaves, hipped or lower pitched roof, rather than anything taller.
- Keep the east wall as far west as the plot allows, and use the width on the west side if the plot permits.
- Hip the roof end facing No.1 so the ridge is further away.
- Consider adding or enlarging a window to the bedroom on another wall (you own No.1; check permitted development).
