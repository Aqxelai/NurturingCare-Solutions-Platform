## Point 4 – Building line
Measure the front wall of each neighbour from a fixed datum (the back of footway). Draw the line
across the plot. Then: aligned depth = plot depth − (offset between the proposed and aligned
fronts). Minimum footprint per floor from NDSS GIA ÷ number of storeys, plus wall thickness
(GIA is measured internally). Examples: 2-storey 3b5p (93 m²) needs at least 46.5 m² per floor
plus walls; single-storey 2b3p (61 m²) needs at least 61 m² plus walls.

## Point 5 – BRE 209 (3rd edition, 2022) tests
1. **25° test:** in section perpendicular to the window, from the centre of the lowest window
   (confirm height from survey), the obstruction angle is atan((eaves − window centre) ÷
   distance). Passes if no more than 25°, i.e. (eaves − window centre) ÷ distance ≤ 0.466.
2. **VSC:** passes if the window has at least 27% or at least 0.8 times its former value.
3. **Daylight distribution (NSL):** needs the room layout behind each window.
4. **Sunlight (APSH)** for windows facing within 90° of due south: at least 25% annual probable
   sunlight hours, including at least 5% in winter (21 September – 21 March); a loss is noticeable
   if it falls below those and falls by more than 4 percentage points and below 0.8 times former.
5. **Overshadowing of gardens:** at least 50% of the amenity area should get 2 hours of sun on 21 March.
- Windows facing south onto the plot are the most sunlit, so the loss is likely to be greater
  than for a north-facing window. Bathrooms, stairs and landings are given less weight than
  living rooms, kitchens and bedrooms; the room use matters.
- If tests fail: reduce eaves height, move the house away from No.1, reduce footprint, then
  re-test. Rights of light and party wall matters are civil, not planning, but can stop a build.
- Do not state "no harm" unless the figures show it.

## Occupancy update: 4–5 bedrooms
- 4b5p two-storey needs at least 97 m² GIA (about 49 m² per floor plus walls).
- Any five-bedroom type needs more than 4b5p; check the figures in the Nationally Described Space
  Standard before quoting them.
- A larger house makes points 3–5 harder. Say plainly in the pre-application that this size is the
  hardest case for the plot, and consider asking the officer about a three-bedroom alternative.

## No.1 side windows: rooms served (owner's statement)
- Ground floor: kitchen. First floor: bedroom (the two-light window in the photo).
- BRE 209 counts kitchens and bedrooms as habitable rooms; living rooms, kitchens and bedrooms
  carry more weight than bathrooms or landings. A kitchen's daylight and a bedroom's sunlight
  are both tested.
- If either room has no other window, the side window is its only light, so the loss is more
  significant. Photographs of each room's windows are needed.
